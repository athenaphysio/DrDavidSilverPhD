The person behind the practice

I became a physiotherapist because I was fascinated by how people could move better.

Two decades later, that fascination hasn't dimmed. It's deepened into something I couldn't have predicted: a conviction that movement IS empowerment, and that the most impactful clinical tool I possess is not my education or clinical training, but my ability to make someone feel genuinely understood and capable.

"David did it the hard way. 10 years at the sharp end of education behaviour change research in sport. All pointless if it cannot be put to use. That use is creating ideas, different outlooks and providing energy."

I spent a decade in research because I wanted to understand, at the deepest level, why people change — and why they don't. The answers weren't where medicine expected to find them. They were in the relationship between clinician and patient. In the moment a person stops being a diagnosis and starts being a human being who feels genuinely heard.

That insight — earned through tens of thousands of hours of clinical work — is what I bring into every room I enter. Whether I'm treating a patient in Richmond, speaking to a boardroom of senior leaders, or preparing a medico-legal report, the same conviction underpins every piece of the work.

People don't change because they're told what's good for them. They change when someone they trust translates information into something that's personally meaningful.

My wife works in HR. My sister is an HR Director. Years of conversations with them — at the end of long days, in the quiet moments when the professional mask comes off — showed me something I hadn't seen clearly before: the people most responsible for organisational wellbeing are often the least supported themselves. That realisation fostered a drive to understand more, and it became the foundation of a new direction in my work.

I now work at the intersection of clinical science and human performance — in consulting rooms, gyms and pitches, in corporate boardrooms, in court, and increasingly, in the research that shapes how organisations think about the people who run them.

The journey

Built over two decades and still growing and learning.

1999

Sport Science BSc (Hons)

An unrelenting passion for sport becomes an academic vocation. A fire to understand human performance is lit which has never gone out.

2007

Qualified as a Chartered Physiotherapist

Graduated and registered with the CSP and HCPC. Began clinical practice at the sharp end of musculoskeletal medicine, the foundation of everything that followed.

2008

Early specialism in Sports Medicine — MSc Sport & Exercise Rehabilitation

Developed a clinical focus on performance, injury rehabilitation and the relationship between physical capacity and human behaviour. Began working with athletes across a range of sports.

2009

The Forge Clinic, Richmond

Joined The Forge Clinic in Richmond upon Thames building a clinical practice rooted in the conviction that the therapeutic relationship is as important as the therapeutic technique.

2012

Began postgraduate research

Entered doctoral study at St Mary’s University, Twickenham, pursuing questions about concussion in youth rugby that clinical practice alone couldn’t answer. A decade of evidence-based research began.

2019

Corporate wellbeing work begins

First engagements delivering wellbeing sessions for corporate clients including Google Cloud, Citrix and Agilysys. Discovered that the same principles that transform clinical outcomes could transform organisational ones.

2023

PhD awarded — St Mary’s University, Twickenham

Doctoral research completed. BSc, MSc, PhD. A decade of research into concussion, brain health and behaviour change in sport — producing findings that now directly inform clinical and corporate work.

2023

Athena Physiotherapy, Cobham

Founded Athena Physiotherapy in Cobham, Surrey — a second clinical base reflecting a growing practice and a broadening reach across the London commuter belt.

2025

Expert witness practice established

Formalised medico-legal expert witness work in cases involving musculoskeletal injury, concussion and sports medicine. CPR Part 35 compliant. Instructed by solicitors across England and Wales.

2026

HR Wellbeing Research Project launched

Original qualitative research with senior HR professionals — exploring the lived experience of the people most responsible for organisational wellbeing. White paper in preparation.

What makes this different

There are plenty of wellbeing speakers who are expert clinicians. There are plenty who are warm, engaging communicators. Very few are both — and fewer still have done the original research to underpin everything they say.

That combination is what makes David's work genuinely rare in this market. It's the result of twenty years of helping people do hard things.
