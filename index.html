The person behind the practice

Science tells you what.
I help people understand why.

I became a physiotherapist because I was fascinated by how people could move better. Two decades later, that fascination hasn't dimmed. It's deepened into something I couldn't have predicted: a conviction that movement IS empowerment and the most impactful clinical tool I possess is not my education or clinical training, but my ability to make someone feel genuinely understood and capable.

My wife works in HR. My sister is an HR Director. Over years of conversations at the end of long days, they showed me something I hadn’t seen clearly before. The people most responsible for organisational wellbeing are often the least supported themselves. That realisation fostered a drive to understand more.

I now work at the intersection of clinical science and human performance — in consulting rooms, gyms and pitches, in corporate boardrooms, in court, and increasingly, in the research that shapes how organisations think about the people who run them.

What makes the difference

Most wellbeing content informs.
This content empowers.

Information alone has never changed anyone’s behaviour. Emotion does. The clinical science informs, but it lands only because of genuine human connection.

Most corporate wellbeing speakers are one of three things: a clinician who is dry and academic, a motivational speaker with no clinical credibility, or a coach with a personal story but no science behind it. David is none of these.
